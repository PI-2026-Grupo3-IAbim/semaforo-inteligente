Lost Signal

A short story about navigation, technology, and the importance of looking around.

I must have looked like a lunatic. I waved my phone in the air trying to calibrate the compass, took three steps to the left, then two back to the right, while the GPS lady kept repeating in my ear:

“Head southeast.”

Southeast? How was I supposed to know which way southeast was on a Tuesday morning? All this with 3% battery and a hunger that was making me irritable. I needed to find Central Bakery to meet a friend, but the little blue dot on the screen only went around in circles.

In front of me there was only a huge gray wall and an old man leaning against a newspaper stand, drinking coffee from a plastic cup and staring at me like I was an alien.

“Did you drop some money on the ground, son?” he asked, blowing on his coffee.

“I wish. I'm trying to find this darn bakery, but the app has gone crazy. It says I'm here, but all I can see is this gray wall,” I said without looking away from the screen.

The old man chuckled quietly, shook his head, and pointed over his shoulder with his plastic cup:

“Well, if you lift your head two inches, you'll see that gray wall is the side of the bakery. The door is right there around the corner. Just follow the smell of toasted bread.”

I looked up. There it was. A huge yellow sign right above me, the door open, and the delicious smell of freshly brewed coffee filling the sidewalk.

At that exact moment, my phone screen blinked and went dark for good. I stood there staring at the black mirror of my dead phone and started laughing with the old man. We invent satellites, apps, and artificial intelligence, but we still need to lift our heads so we don't walk into a wall.
